# class coverage and the coexistence case

## the scope principle

trademark rights are not ownership of a word; they are rights in a
word as applied to specific goods and services, grouped by nice
classes. the existing medical-device "sculptura" (class 10) and
galderma's "sculptra" (pharmaceutical/medical) are different lanes
from ours:

- our goods: jewelry (class 14)
- our services: software design tools (classes 9, 42), marketplace
  retail services (class 35), manufacturing for third parties
  (class 40)

## the coexistence case, stated plainly

likelihood of confusion is the test (in the us, the du pont
factors). working through the ones that matter here:

- **goods/services relatedness**: medical devices and radiation
  therapy have nothing to do with jewelry or creative software.
  consumers do not encounter both in one purchasing context. this
  is the strongest factor in our favor.
- **channels and customers**: hospitals/dermatology clinics versus
  consumer marketplace shoppers and jewelry creators. disjoint.
- **mark strength**: "sculptura" is suggestive-coined (sculpture
  root), not famous-in-our-lane; the existing holders have no
  presence in our space.
- **similarity of the marks**: identical word to the sensus mark,
  near-identical to galderma's sculptra. this is the one factor
  against us, and it is why the file exists: identical spelling
  raises the stakes of the other factors being genuinely disjoint,
  which they are.

## honest adjacency notes

1. **the beauty halo**: jewelry and medical aesthetics both sell
   into self-presentation. no court case treats that as related
   commerce, but galderma is large, litigious-by-capacity, and
   their opposition in a filing window is a real (if unlikely)
   outcome. counsel prices this.
2. **fame risk is one-directional**: we are the small party. our
   exposure is an opposition proceeding at filing time, not an
   infringement suit later, as long as we stay in our lanes and
   never sell into theirs (we never sell medical anything).
3. **the name choice already cleared our own bar**: coined,
   distinctive in our lane, and now confirmed non-famous in
   jewelry/consumer. the medical registrations do not change the
   brand decision; they just require the formal steps in
   [clearance-and-filing-plan.md](clearance-and-filing-plan.md).

## if the unlikely bad outcome happens

if counsel reads real opposition risk (galderma objecting to our
class 9/42 filings, say), the options in order of preference:
carve-outs (limit our class descriptions to exclude anything
aesthetic/medical), coexistence agreement, or narrowly renaming
the product surfaces while keeping the company name. the platform
architecture is name-agnostic (handles, storefronts, domains swap
cheaply pre-launch); that was a quiet benefit of the bare-name
choice.
